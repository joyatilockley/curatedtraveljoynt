The Guide

Seven mistakes. Every one of them avoidable.

01
Timing
Booking Last Minute
Inventory moves fast. Airlines cancel routes. Fuel costs are driving price spikes that did not exist two years ago. The traveler who waits pays more for less, has fewer room category options, and sometimes finds that the property they wanted is simply not available at any price.
With current global fuel disruptions affecting jet fuel supply, flight prices are not going down. The window to lock in favorable rates is earlier than most people think.
Fix it Book at least 90 to 120 days out for international travel. For peak season, holiday weeks, or high-demand destinations, six months is not too early.
02
Airport Preparation
Showing Up to the Airport Unprepared
No TSA PreCheck. No Global Entry for international travel. No awareness that TSA now offers touchless PreCheck enrollment. Travelers lose 30 to 45 minutes in security lines that did not need to cost them anything. That time adds stress before the trip even begins.
TSA PreCheck costs $78 for five years. Global Entry is $100 and includes PreCheck. Either one pays for itself the first time you walk past a line of 200 people.
Fix it Apply for PreCheck or Global Entry before your next international trip. Enroll using the new touchless option. Do it once and stop thinking about airport lines for five years.
03
Booking Method
Using a Third Party Platform
Expedia, Hotels.com, and Google Flights will give you a rate. What they will not give you is a relationship or an advocate. When your flight is cancelled, your room is not what you booked, or something goes wrong at 11pm in a foreign country, you are on hold with a call center that does not know your name and has no leverage with the property.
Third party platforms also strip away the ability to apply loyalty benefits, request room upgrades, or communicate special circumstances to a property in advance.
Fix it Book through an advisor who has direct relationships with properties and suppliers. You are not paying more. You are paying for protection and access.
04
Property Research
Assuming All-Inclusive Means All-Inclusive
It does not. The fine print on all-inclusive properties varies dramatically. The swim-up bar may be a separate tier. The specialty restaurants may require reservations that were fully booked before you arrived. The room marketed as ocean view may face a parking structure from a different angle than the photos suggest.
Most travelers do not read the property details until they are already standing at the front desk. By then the options are limited and the disappointment is real.
Fix it Read every word of the property tier structure before you book. Ask specific questions. Or work with an advisor who already knows the answers.
05
Budgeting
Underestimating What the Trip Actually Costs
Resort fees. Gratuities. Airport transfers. Travel protection. These are standard line items most travelers forget when they build a budget. And then there are the destination-specific surprises that catch nearly everyone off guard.
Mexico charges an Environmental Sanitation Rights tax that is completely separate from your resort fees and anything on your booking confirmation. It is not optional. It is not small. And almost no one knows about it until they check out.
Fix it Budget 20 to 30 percent above your base booking cost for destination-specific taxes, fees, transfers, and gratuities. Ask your advisor what is not included before you confirm.
06
Protection
Skipping Travel Protection
There is no version of international travel where skipping travel protection is the right financial decision. Cancelled flights, medical situations, natural events, and political disruptions are not rare edge cases. They happen on ordinary trips to ordinary destinations every single day. The travelers who get stranded are always the ones who thought it would not happen to them.
Travel protection is not a luxury. It is the cost of protecting everything else you spent on the trip.
Fix it Purchase travel protection at the time of booking, not after. Coverage rules are time-sensitive. Your advisor can walk you through the right policy for your specific trip.
07
The Real Mistake
Planning It Alone
Every mistake in this guide is what happens when a traveler navigates the planning process without expert support. Not because they are not smart. Because the information required to plan a truly protected, curated international trip is not visible on any booking platform. It lives in relationships, insider access, and years of pattern recognition.
You hire an accountant for your taxes. You hire a doctor for your health. The question is not whether a travel advisor is worth it. The question is why you were doing it alone.
